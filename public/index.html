Good to know

E-paper takes a few seconds to redraw and keeps the picture with the power off. That's why the display sleeps between updates — and why you can't animate it.

Configure Adafruit IO

Your board reads its picture from Adafruit IO and reports back on the same account. This makes the feeds it needs.

Adafruit IO key

Four feeds, under the group —

bitmap — the picture your board draws
sleep — how long it waits before waking
status — what your board reports back
canvas-state — the editable scene, so any browser can open it

Changing the key will change API URLs and MQTT subscription topics…

Where this lands
What a Group is

A Group is a folder for feeds on Adafruit IO. This display gets one, named after it, holding the four feeds it needs: the picture it draws, how long it sleeps, what it reports back, and the editable scene behind the picture.

Where to find your key

Your Adafruit IO key is on io.adafruit.com, behind the yellow key icon. It is stored in this browser and written onto the device when you flash it — treat it like a password.

If it already exists

We'll check to see if you already have a group and feeds configured for this device. If it's already configured, we'll do nothing and we'll let you know!

Nothing here is destructive. An existing group is reused as it stands, and a feed that is already there is left alone — we only ever add what's missing.

Which Wi-Fi should the device join?

Your board needs its own connection — it fetches its picture over the network long after this browser tab is closed.

Password

Most boards join 2.4 GHz networks only. A dual-band router that broadcasts one name for both works fine — the board picks the band it can see.

What happens to these

The network name and password are used once, in this browser, to write credentials onto the device over USB.

They are not sent to Adafruit IO, not stored in your account, and not kept after this step. Leave this screen and you will be asked again.

Why we ask

The board is on its own once it is flashed. It wakes, joins your network, reads the picture you pushed from Adafruit IO, draws it and goes back to sleep — none of which it can do without these.

Changing networks later

Run this step again over USB. Because nothing is stored, moving a board to a new network means plugging it back in — there is no way to push credentials to a board that cannot reach the network yet.

What won't work

Captive portals — hotel, campus and most guest networks — need a browser to accept terms before traffic passes. Your board has no browser, so it will associate and then fail to reach Adafruit IO.

Before you connect

          

            
1 Use a data cable. A charge-only USB cable powers the board and carries no serial — it is the single most common reason no port appears.

            
2 Close anything else holding the port: the Arduino IDE's serial monitor, screen, Mu, Thonny.

            
3 Pick your board in the browser's port dialog. On macOS it is usually a usbmodem; on Windows a COM port.

            
4 If nothing is listed, put the board in bootloader mode: hold BOOT, tap RESET, release BOOT.

          

          

          
What gets written

          
The marquee firmware for your board, your Adafruit IO configuration, the network configuration from the last step, and the panel configuration. Existing files on the board are erased.

          

          
Browser support

          
Flashing needs WebSerial: Chrome, Edge or Opera on desktop. Safari and Firefox cannot connect to a serial port at all, and there is no extension that adds it.

Settings

      
    


    Adafruit IO
    

      
      
      
    

    
    
The device's Adafruit IO group. Its feeds are {group}.bitmap, {group}.sleep, {group}.status and {group}.canvas-state.


    
    
    
Off, everything talks to io.adafruit.com. On, it all moves to the .us staging environment — feed reads, publishes, the group A5b creates, and the host written into the code bundle. The two are separate accounts with separate keys, so switching mid-setup will need different credentials.


    

    ProtoMQ target
    
Display publishing uses the topic {user}/ws-b2d/{device}.

    

      
      
    


    

    Sleep behaviour
    

      
      
    

    
Duration is the timer the device sleeps on, and is what "Wake and redraw" in the editor sets. It also picks the sleep mode, which is why there is no control for one: under 5 minutes the device light-sleeps, because waking in place beats paying for a boot; from 5 minutes up it deep-sleeps. The retry window is how long "Push to display" keeps resending the image (every 45s) waiting for the device to confirm the write; sleep is only sent once it does.

              Presets
              

            


            

              
              
              
              
              
              
              
              
            


            
Column offset shifts the framebuffer against the controller's column RAM, in pixels — leave it blank unless the panel has a dead zone at one edge. The 2.13" Tri-Color FeatherWing needs 8; the SSD1680Z breakout needs -8. Blank or 0 is left out of cfg-marquee.json, where absent means no shift.

A pin of -1 means "not wired".
